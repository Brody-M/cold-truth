- **Excluded:** Invented dialogue, caller name, theories about how the caller knew the family, and current DNA material.

### 2. Amy and the ordinary afternoon — 150–180 words

- **Narrative purpose:** Humanize Amy carefully through her age, school departure, Bay Village setting, and official FBI description without inventing biography.

- **Transition:** Amy reached Bay Village Square and appeared to be waiting for the person who had called.
- **Excluded:** Unsupported personality traits, family conflict, private routines, and creator anecdotes.

### 3. The shopping-center encounter — 220–260 words

- **Transition:** When Amy did not return, the documented encounter became the starting point of a major police and FBI investigation.
- **Excluded:** Vehicle claims, suspect identities, lineup claims, polygraph claims, and reconstructions of what happened after the parking lot.

### 4. Search and recovery — 180–220 words

- **Transition:** The recovery answered where Amy was found, but the unrecovered property and other evidence created new questions.
- **Excluded:** Graphic injury detail, probabilistic sexual-assault language, speculative dump timing, and invented search scenes.

### 5. Evidence that remained unidentified — 230–280 words

- **Transition:** As forensic tools improved, investigators returned to physical material for another possible path.
- **Excluded:** Repeating the full item list elsewhere, claiming the curtain identifies a crime scene, or attaching any item to a named person.

### 6. DNA efforts and present status — 190–230 words

- **Claims:** AM-09, AM-10, AM-13, AM-14, AM-15.
- **Distinct contribution:** Provides a genuine modern investigative development and an accurate present-day status without promising a breakthrough.
- **Transition:** Return from technical limitations to the unresolved human loss and official request for information.
- **Excluded:** Suspect DNA language, genetic-genealogy claims not confirmed in the accepted sources, and assertions that a resolution is close.

### 7. Closing — 80–100 words

- **Narrative purpose:** Return to Amy as a ten-year-old child and close on the continuing official investigation with a neutral CTA.
- **Claims:** AM-01, AM-09, AM-10.
- **Distinct contribution:** Provides a respectful human conclusion without recycling the hook or evidence list.
- **Transition:** Final cadence.
- **Excluded:** Theory invitations, named suspects, audience sleuthing requests, graphic language, and sensational framing.
