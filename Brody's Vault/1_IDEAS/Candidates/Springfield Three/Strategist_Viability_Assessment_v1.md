## Distinct-information check

Every chapter introduces a different function and fact set:

- Chapter 1 defines the case and official time window.
- Chapter 2 supplies identity, relationships, and graduation context.
- Chapter 3 advances the verified final-evening chronology.
- Chapter 4 introduces the condition of the residence and property left behind.
- Chapter 5 covers search scale and investigative burden.
- Chapter 6 supplies current status and long-term human impact.
- Chapter 7 closes without adding or recycling a theory.

The Writer and Editor must still run a repetition audit. Lists of belongings, the phrase “active investigation,” and the disappearance window should each be fully explained once and used only as concise callbacks afterward.

## Material excluded from the architecture

- Named suspects, convicted-offender comparisons, alleged confessions, jailhouse statements, and psychic claims: uncorroborated and legally/reputationally unsafe.
- The suspicious-van lead and anonymous calls or letters: investigative leads, not established facts or explanations.
- The hospital parking-structure theory: publicly circulated rumor; unnecessary to the core narrative and not evidence of what happened.
- The broken porch-light globe and erased-answering-machine story: not sufficiently verified within the approved source boundary.
- Claims that police negligence, scene contamination, or misconduct caused the case to remain unsolved: unsupported by the current ledger and would require primary records plus a fair official response.
- Legal-death assertions: excluded until the relevant court orders are obtained.
- Specific party addresses, private conversations, routes, and personality details: not established in the approved claim ledger.
- Any invitation for viewers to name a suspect or propose a theory: inconsistent with the source threshold and Shorts/long-form safety rules.
