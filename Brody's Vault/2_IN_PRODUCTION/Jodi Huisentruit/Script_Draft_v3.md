## 1. HOOK

At roughly 4:10 on the morning of June 27, 1995, a colleague called Jodi Huisentruit. The KIMT morning anchor had not arrived for work.

Three hours later, police were dispatched to her apartment complex. By then, a missed shift had become something far more serious. Jodi was gone, and more than thirty years later, the investigation into her disappearance remains active.

## 2. ESSENTIAL SETUP

Jodi worked as a morning anchor for KIMT in Mason City, Iowa. On a normal workday, the station expected her to be there for its early broadcast. On June 27, she was not.

That absence was the first clear warning. It was not based on a rumor or a later reconstruction. Jodi had a place she was supposed to be, and the people working with her knew she had not arrived.

[B-ROLL: empty local-news anchor desk before sunrise, control-room monitors glowing in a quiet studio]

The first documented attempt to reach her came at roughly 4:10 that morning.

## 3. THE MORNING SHE VANISHED

A KIMT colleague called Jodi at about 4:10 a.m. Public reporting establishes that contact, but the available record does not provide a complete account of the conversation. What is clear is that Jodi still did not arrive at the station.

As the morning continued, concern moved beyond the workplace. According to the public dispatch report, police were sent to Jodi's apartment complex for a welfare check at 7:13 a.m. That time fixes the official response within the morning's sequence. It cannot tell us what happened during the hours before officers were dispatched.

At the apartment parking lot, the situation became more troubling. Public reporting described drag marks beside Jodi's car. It also described belongings left in the area: a bent key for her Miata, red heels, earrings, a blow dryer, and hair spray.

[B-ROLL: rain-darkened apartment parking lot followed by restrained case-card text listing the reported belongings]

Those items mattered because their reported presence made the missed shift more concerning. They connected the unanswered absence to the parking lot beside Jodi's car. But they did not establish who was responsible, what exactly occurred, or where Jodi had gone.

The parking lot gave investigators a scene to examine. It did not give them the answer they needed most.

[CLAIMS: JH-02, JH-03, JH-04]

## 4. EVIDENCE AND INVESTIGATION

Jodi's case remained open as the years passed. Iowa's Missing Person Information Clearinghouse continued to list her and directed information to Mason City Police or the Iowa Division of Criminal Investigation.

Later records show two different investigative steps.

In 2017, a search warrant sought GPS data connected to two vehicles. Part of that warrant was unsealed in 2025, but the supporting affidavit remained sealed because the investigation was ongoing. The public portion confirms the request for historical location information, but the sealed affidavit prevents responsible conclusions about its basis or result.

[B-ROLL: neutral legal papers and a sealed envelope on a desk, with no invented names or document text]

In November 2025, Mason City Police searched an area in Winsted, Minnesota. The department said the search was part of the ongoing investigation and was not prompted by a new tip. Police also said nothing significant was found.

That result matters because investigative activity can sound like a breakthrough when it first becomes public. In this instance, police were explicit: the search was part of continuing case work, and it did not produce a significant finding.

The warrant and the Winsted search show that the investigation did not simply stop. One involved an effort to obtain location data; the other involved examining a physical area. Neither supplied a public explanation for Jodi's disappearance.

## 5. WHERE THE CASE STANDS

Jodi remains listed as missing by the State of Iowa. At the event marking the 31st anniversary of her disappearance in June 2026, the lead investigator said the case remained active.

[B-ROLL: quiet Mason City neighborhood at dusk, followed by a simple calendar turning from 1995 to the present]

Active does not mean that investigators have announced a solution. The 2025 search found nothing significant, and part of the warrant record remains sealed. The approved public sources do not explain what happened to Jodi or where she is.

What they do establish is narrower but important: the case has not been closed, official agencies still list Jodi as missing, and the investigation continues more than three decades after the morning she failed to arrive at work.

## 6. CLOSING

Jodi's story entered the public record through an ordinary expectation: she was supposed to arrive at work. Her absence became a search that has lasted for decades.

She remains more than a name in an open file. If you value careful, source-based coverage of cases that still need attention, you can follow Cold Truth for the next story.
