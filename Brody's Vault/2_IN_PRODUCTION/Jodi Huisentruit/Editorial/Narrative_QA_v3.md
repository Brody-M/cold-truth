**Yes.** The draft gives one forward sequence:

1. Jodi was expected at KIMT and did not arrive.
2. A colleague called at roughly 4:10 a.m.
3. She still did not arrive.
4. Police were dispatched for a welfare check at 7:13 a.m.
5. Reporting described drag marks and belongings beside her car.
6. The evidence created an investigative scene but no public explanation.
7. Later actions included a GPS-data warrant and the Winsted search.
8. Neither produced a public resolution; Jodi remains listed as missing and the case was described as active in June 2026.

The script does not backtrack to retell the morning after the investigation begins.

The statement that the case remains active supplies present stakes; the later status section explains what that official status means.

## Does every evidence detail explain why it matters?

**Yes, within the ledger boundary.**

- **4:10 a.m. call:** establishes an early documented contact point after Jodi failed to arrive.
- **7:13 a.m. dispatch:** fixes the official response time without claiming what occurred beforehand.
- **Drag marks and belongings:** explain why the situation was more concerning than an ordinary missed shift, without asserting an attack or abduction.
- **GPS-data warrant:** shows investigators sought historical vehicle-location information; the sealed affidavit limits public interpretation.
- **Winsted search:** shows physical investigative activity continued decades later; the police statement establishes that it was not prompted by a new tip and found nothing significant.
- **Official missing-person listing and active-case statement:** establish the verified present status.
