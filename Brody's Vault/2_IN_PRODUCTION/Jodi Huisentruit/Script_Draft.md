## CASE INTRO

Jodi Huisentruit worked as a morning anchor for KIMT. On June 27, 1995, she did not arrive for her shift.

The confirmed sequence is limited. A colleague called her at roughly 4:10 a.m. When she still had not arrived, a welfare check was requested. According to the public dispatch record, police were sent to her apartment complex at 7:13 a.m.

[B-ROLL: newsroom control-room monitors before a broadcast, empty anchor desk, soft blue light]

The available reporting describes physical evidence in the parking lot. It does not provide a public answer to the question that has remained ever since: what happened to Jodi?

That limit matters. A missing-person case can leave room for theories. This episode stays with the documented timeline and the facts that can be attributed.

The difference between a confirmed time and a presumed explanation matters. The call, the missed shift, the welfare check, and the reported items are documented. The reason Jodi disappeared, and what followed after that morning, are not. That is the boundary of this account.

## THE INCIDENT

The early-morning call is where the public timeline begins. Jodi had not arrived at work. Later that morning, police were dispatched to her apartment complex for a welfare check.

[B-ROLL: generic emergency-dispatch console, hands typing, no identifiable agency or screen details]

Reporting later described drag marks and several personal items in the parking lot. They included a red pair of heels, earrings, a blow dryer, hair spray, and a bent key for Jodi's Miata.

[EDIT: Kept the item list but removed repeated explanations of what it cannot prove.]

Those details are part of the public record. They do not identify a person responsible, and they do not tell us where Jodi went after leaving home for work.

What can be said is that a local news anchor was expected at work that morning, and within a few hours, a welfare check had been dispatched to her apartment complex.

## THE INVESTIGATION

Iowa's Missing Person Information Clearinghouse still lists Jodi and directs anyone with information to Mason City Police or the Iowa Division of Criminal Investigation.

[B-ROLL: archived-looking missing-person flyer on a bulletin board, camera slowly pulls back]

Public updates show that investigators have continued to pursue leads. In November 2025, Mason City Police searched an area in Winsted, Minnesota. The department said the search was part of the ongoing investigation, not the result of a new tip, and that nothing significant was found.

There is also a limit on what the public can review. A 2017 search warrant seeking GPS data connected to two vehicles was partly unsealed in 2025. The warrant was connected to John Vansice's vehicles, but the affidavit remained sealed because the investigation was ongoing.

That connection does not establish guilt. The sealed affidavit is not evidence the public can interpret or use to fill in the unanswered parts of the case.

The public updates do show that the investigation has continued. They do not turn an open question into a conclusion. In a case this old, restraint is part of accuracy: a search, a warrant, or a public connection can be significant without proving what happened.

## RESOLUTION OR CURRENT STATUS

There is no resolution to offer.

At the 31st anniversary of Jodi's disappearance in June 2026, the lead investigator said the case remained active. That does not promise an answer. It does mean the case has not been closed and investigators are still asking for information.

Active is not the same as solved. It means the official record still leaves room for information that could matter, even after decades have passed.

For the people who knew Jodi, the central fact has not changed. She is still missing.

The public record gives us times on a clock, a parking lot, and the items left behind. The rest remains unknown.

If you have information about Jodi Huisentruit's disappearance, contact the Mason City Police Department or the Iowa Division of Criminal Investigation.

Jodi should be remembered as more than an unanswered case.
