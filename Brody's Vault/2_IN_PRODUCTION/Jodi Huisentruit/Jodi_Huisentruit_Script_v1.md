## CASE INTRO

Jodi Huisentruit worked as a morning anchor for KIMT. On the morning of June 27, 1995, she did not arrive for her shift.

The basic outline is painfully simple. A colleague called her at roughly 4:10 a.m. Later, concern grew. At 7:13 a.m., according to the public dispatch record, a welfare check was sent to her apartment complex.

[B-ROLL: newsroom control-room monitors before a broadcast, empty anchor desk, soft blue light]

Public reporting described physical evidence in the parking lot. But the public record does not answer the question that matters most: what happened to Jodi after that morning?

That distinction matters. In cases like this, the absence of a public answer can invite people to fill the space with theories. Cold Truth will not do that here. We are following the documented timeline, not trying to invent the missing pieces.

## THE INCIDENT

The confirmed timeline begins with that early call from a KIMT colleague. Jodi had not arrived at work. After she still did not appear, a welfare check was requested.

At 7:13 a.m., dispatch records show that police were sent to the apartment complex.

[B-ROLL: generic emergency-dispatch console, hands typing, no identifiable agency or screen details]

In the parking lot, reporting later described drag marks and several personal items. They included a red pair of heels, earrings, a blow dryer, hair spray, and a bent key for Jodi's Miata.

Those are the details that have been publicly reported. They are not a complete account of what happened. They do not establish who was responsible. And they do not tell us where Jodi went after she left home for work.

What they do show is the suddenness of the morning. A local news anchor was expected at work. Within a few hours, a welfare check had been dispatched to her apartment complex.

## THE INVESTIGATION

The case did not simply disappear into a file. Iowa's Missing Person Information Clearinghouse still lists Jodi, and directs anyone with information to Mason City Police or the Iowa Division of Criminal Investigation.

[B-ROLL: archived-looking missing-person flyer on a bulletin board, camera slowly pulls back]

Public updates also show that investigators have continued to pursue leads long after the initial search.

In November 2025, Mason City Police searched an area in Winsted, Minnesota. The department said the search was part of the ongoing investigation, not the result of a new tip. It also said that nothing significant was found.

That is the difficult rhythm of a case like this. A search may be part of an ongoing investigation and still yield no public breakthrough.

[B-ROLL: winter trees and an empty rural property line; no homes or addresses identifiable]

There is another important limit on what the public knows. A 2017 search warrant seeking GPS data connected to two vehicles was partly unsealed in 2025. The warrant was connected to John Vansice's vehicles. But the affidavit remained sealed because the investigation was ongoing.

The fact that a warrant exists does not establish guilt. And the sealed affidavit is not a blank space for anyone else to fill with speculation. It is simply information the public does not have.

## RESOLUTION OR CURRENT STATUS

There is no resolution to offer here.

As of the 31st anniversary of Jodi's disappearance in June 2026, the lead investigator said the case remained active. That does not guarantee an answer. But it means the case has not been closed, and the people responsible for investigating it are still asking for information.

[B-ROLL: one porch light turning on at dusk, then a wider quiet neighborhood view]

For Jodi's family, colleagues, and friends, the passage of time has not changed the central fact. She is still missing.

The public record preserves a few times on a clock, a parking lot, and the items left behind. The rest of the story remains unknown.

If you have information about Jodi Huisentruit's disappearance, contact the Mason City Police Department or the Iowa Division of Criminal Investigation.

Cases like this one do not fade just because the years pass. Jodi's name is still on an active case file. And until there are answers, she should be remembered as more than a mystery.
